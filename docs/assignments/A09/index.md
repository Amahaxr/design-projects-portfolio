# A9 – Pulleys

## Objective

Design a belt-driven pulley drive system that transfers torque from one parallel shaft to another. For this purpose, design a system using two pulleys of diameter 3 inches, center distance between 4 and 6 inches, speed range of 1000 to 2000 rpm, and a belt tension of 4 to 6 pounds force.

The current project will focus more on belt selection, shaft design, journal bearing design, and structure of the frame, which must have a minimum safety factore of 3.

## Analyze

Part 1 - Belt Design 

Belt tension, pulley geometry, friction, and the minimum belt cross-section were considered. For evaluation the belt size, the tensile strength of the polyurethane rubber is 471 psi and a 5-inch pulley center distance is used. 

This belt design is based on the desired circumferences using the McMaster-Carr round belt catoligue.

Part 2 - Shaft Design

Shafts made of AISI 1060 harden steel were analyzed under combined bending and twisting forces. Bearing reactions, bending stress, torsional shear stress, minimum shaft diameter, and the actual safety factor were calculated.

Calculation data:

Minimum shaft diameter: 0.1100 in

Shaft diameter chosen: 0.125 in

Minimum cross-sectional area: 0.00951 in^2

Safety factor calculation: 4.40

Part 3 - Journal Bearing Design

Evaluated porous bronze journal bearings using the bearing pressure, shaft surface velocity and PV number.

Evaluation was done at the following operating conditions: rotational speed = 1500 rpm, shaft diameter = 0.125 inches and bearing length = 0.50 inches.

Computed values:

Bearing load = 5 lbs/bearing

Bearing pressure = 80 psi

Surface velocity = 49.09 ft/min

PV value = 3,927 psi times ft / min

Part 4 - Frame Design

Designing a preliminary aluminum frame with two plates to align pulleys and support the shaft bearings.

Selected dimensions:

Material = 6061 - T6 aluminum

Length of the plate = 9.00 inches

Width of the plate = 1.00 inch

Thickness of the plate = 0.50 inch 

Center distance of pulleys = 5.00 inches

Evaluating nominal compressive stress, bearing hole pressure, axial deformation and safety factor.

Frame design is a preliminary static analysis. Other parameters that need to be checked include bearing fits and connection fasteners.

## Decide

Selected polyurethane round belt driven system at the speed of 1500 rpm, with 3 inches diameter pulleys at an interval of 5 inches.

Selection of AISI 1060 steel for shafts based on the calculation of bending and torsional stresses. Porous bronze selected for journal bearings based on the calculations of pressure and PV values.

Selected 6061 - T6 aluminum alloy for the frame due to its machinability and light weight in the application of a basic framework design.

The design calculations meet the nominal strengths and bearing requirements of the selected material. More work needs to be done in order to validate the entire system.

## Communicate

Part 5 - Engineering Lessons Learned

Some of the engineering lessons learned during the implementation of this power transmission design project including the following.

Belt Design: With respect to design, I have learned about how the belt's tension and friction influence the torque transfer and the permissible tensile stress of the polyurethane belt determines the cross-sectional area

Shaft Design: The effects of bending and torsional stresses in a shaft and the application of bon Mises failure criterion to determine the minimum shaft diameter have been learned in shaft design.

Journal Bearing: The fact that bearing selection depends on more than just load capacity. One should consider the pressure, shaft surface velocity and the PV rating to determine whether the bearing material is suitable.

Frame Design: Material properties, dimensions of the frame, and position of the bearings are some of the factors that influence structural stresses and deformations in a frame. In addition, I have learned that checking only the nominal stress is not sufficient when verifying a frame with holes and mechanical connections.

Overall, this assignment has helped me appreciate the consistency in belt, shaft, bearing and frame calculations.
